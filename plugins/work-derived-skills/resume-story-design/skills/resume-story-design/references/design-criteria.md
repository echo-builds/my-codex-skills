# Resume design criteria

Use these criteria when diagnosing or redesigning a resume. They are decision principles, not a fixed template.

## Positioning and story

- The name, target role, and strongest evidence should be discoverable within the first scan.
- Organize experience around what the candidate discovered, decided, built, changed, or proved.
- Keep claim strength proportional to evidence. Distinguish launched results, offline validation, prototypes, and unverified hypotheses.
- Make chronology, continuation, and project ownership unambiguous.

## Hierarchy

- Keep the number of competing visual levels low. Three major levels are usually enough: section, experience/project, body/evidence.
- Give company and representative project names more emphasis than dates or administrative metadata.
- Use a single accent color consistently. Do not create hierarchy by introducing several near-identical text colors.
- Prefer weight and spacing over decorative bars, boxes, or pills. Use backgrounds only when they create a meaningful group or reliable scan target.

## Typography

- Choose a font with strong Chinese readability and stable PDF rendering.
- Body text must remain readable at normal A4 viewing size; do not solve page pressure by repeatedly shrinking type.
- Keep paragraph line height compact but not crowded. Avoid isolated punctuation, percentages, or short fragments on their own line.
- Use consistent punctuation, number formats, spaces, date formats, and percentage notation.

## Grid and spacing

- Define a small number of shared alignment axes and use them throughout.
- Repeated structures should use the same internal spacing unless meaning requires a difference.
- Section spacing should communicate grouping: tighter within an item, wider between items, and widest between sections.
- Treat unexplained blank space and compressed bottom content as symptoms of the same page-budget problem.
- Prefer fluid content flow and stable grouping over hard-coded vertical offsets.

## Color and decoration

- Default to dark neutral body text and one role-appropriate accent color.
- Use accent color for names, project titles, links, nodes, and meaningful relational cues.
- Avoid large saturated blocks unless the content genuinely needs a strong entry point.
- Every line, node, border, and background should explain structure or improve scanning.

## Role adaptation

- Product and AI product roles: prioritize judgment, user problem, solution logic, ownership, evidence, and results. Use restrained visual language.
- Design roles: allow stronger typography, composition, and portfolio cues while preserving scanability.
- Engineering and data roles: prioritize technical scope, impact, systems, and measurable evidence; visual styling should remain quiet.
- Early-career candidates: education and internships may carry more weight. Experienced candidates: work history and representative outcomes should dominate.
